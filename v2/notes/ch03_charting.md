# Ch 3 — Charting (pp. 79–150)

## Why patterns exist
- Markets have "character" (habits): S&P makes new highs then falls back, fast drops/slow rises; bonds long trends; cattle V-bottoms. → **Patterns are market-specific; test per sector, not assume universal.**
- Behavioural causes: round-number orders (₹100, ₹50 levels cluster orders), public enters late ("grand rush"), momentum overshoot ("lost motion", up to ~10% past target), herd psychology (Mackay).
- Self-fulfilling: many traders act on same obvious patterns → obvious S/R works best.
- Chart scale (log vs linear) and aspect ratio change perceived angles.

## Big drivers of long trends
Government/central-bank policy (rates), international trade (currency), expectations (confidence), supply/demand.

## Dow Theory (6 tenets)
1. Averages discount everything (except acts of God) — averaging removes single-stock manipulation/illiquidity.
2. Three trends: **primary** (years; bull/bear), **secondary** reactions (3 weeks–3 months, retrace part of primary), **minor** (<6 days, noise, manipulable).
   - Bull/bear signal = swing breakout: bull confirmed when price exceeds the previous rally high; bear when it breaks the previous decline low. Convention: 20% reversal from high/low starts bear/bull; swings ~10%.
   - Asymmetry: 20% off a high is many more points than 20% off a low → bias toward bull signals.
   - Bull phases: accumulation → rising participation/volume → speculative blow-off. Bear: distribution → panic → apathy.
   - Secondary reaction: faster than primary; if volume on the decline ≥ prior volume → bear likely; falling volume → just a reaction. A "line" = 2–3 weeks+ sideways in ~5% range.
3. **Confirmation**: two of the averages must agree (breadth of the move). Modern analogue: index + related markets (rates, currency) or sector + index.
4. **Volume goes with the trend**; peaks at bull top/bear panic. Volume falling ~3 months before the top = divergence.
5. Only **closing prices** (settlement = consensus value).
6. **Trend persists until a reversal is signalled** → foundation of all trend following.
- Schabacker end-of-bull: volume surge, narrow leadership, high rates, public talk, warnings. End-of-bear: low volume, falling commodities & rates, low earnings, universal bad news.

## Trendlines, support/resistance, channels
- Uptrend line across rising lows; downtrend across falling highs. More touches = more important. Lines are constantly redrawn.
- **Horizontal support/resistance** = most important lines. Close back inside after penetrating = failed breakout (confirms range). **Broken resistance becomes support** (and vice versa).
- Use **unadjusted prices** for historical S/R levels (splits/back-adjustment create fake levels). ← relevant: adjusted NSE data for returns, raw data for S/R levels traders actually saw.
- Penetration confirmation options: wait n bars, wait for a pullback, or require a band/buffer beyond the line. Delay improves average entry but misses the biggest no-pullback breakouts (which pay for many small losses).
- Horizontal breakout trading: buy above resistance, exit below support; risk = channel width; trail exits under successive swing lows (swing defined by min % reversal).
- "Markets move sideways ~80% of the time" → most breakouts fail.
- Two camps: breakouts after **low** volatility more reliable vs after **high** volatility — test it.
- Channel: trendline + parallel through extreme; buy bottom 20% zone, sell top 20% in uptrend; trade only with the trend; horizontal channel = trading range, fade both edges, exit on penetration.

## One-day patterns
- **Gaps** (opening gap = low > prior high): common (fade, expect fill), breakaway (out of a clear range, often not filled — Bulkowski: only 1% (up) / 6% (down) filled within a week), runaway/continuation (11%/10% filled), exhaustion (58%/72% filled). Gaps make stops slip.
- **Spike**: day's high far above surrounding highs.
  `spike(yesterday) = H[1] − highest(H,n)[2] > k·ATR(n)[2] AND H[1] − H > k·ATR(n)[2]`, k ≥ 0.75 (k>1 rarer, better). Spike high becomes resistance.
- **True range** TR = max(H−L, H−C₋₁, C₋₁−L).
- **Island reversal**: bar(s) isolated by gaps both sides.
- **Pivot point (swing high)**: high greater than N bars each side (more bars → more significant, later). Colby: trading breakout of pivot-point channels > entering on the pivot close. Pivot reversal system on DJIA 100 yrs: buy pivot bottom + higher close; sell pivot top + lower close → profitable, ~70 trades/yr.
- Cups (3-bar low in downtrend; buy within 3 days on close above middle bar's high) / caps (mirror).
- **Reversal day**: new high then lower close. **Key reversal (outside reversal)**: new n-day high, low < prior low, close < prior close, in an uptrend:
  `close[1] > SMA(close,n)[1] AND H ≥ highest(H[1],n) AND L < L[1] AND C < C[1]`. Alone it's "strikingly unimpressive"; **filter TR > 1.5×avg prior 20-day TR** greatly improved (heating oil 2005–11).
- **Pring 2-bar reversal**: strong up bar (open near low, close near high) then mirror bar giving it all back; next days stay below midpoint. Stronger with prior trend, wider bars, higher volume, small retracement.
- **Wide-ranging day** (≥2–3× recent range): shock/breakout; expect pause/reversal; close location gives direction. **Outside day** (higher high & lower low). **Inside day** (volatility compression; often end of a move; next move either way).
- Frequent patterns are less reliable alone; combine with context (volatility, trend extremity, volume).

## Continuation patterns
Triangles (symmetric at trend start; ascending/descending mid-trend; ≥2 weeks to 3 months), flags (lean against trend), pennants, wedges (rising wedge in uptrend), run days (confirm trend, no entry use). A triangle apex narrower than normal noise → breakout just restores normal range (false signal).

## Tops and bottoms
- **V-tops/bottoms**: blow-offs driven by shortage + publicity; impossible to anticipate with reasonable risk; high volatility & volume; become major S/R. Elasticity theory: at extreme prices substitutes, competition, idle supply return, consumers avoid → demand vanishes.
- **Double/triple tops/bottoms**: bottoms more orderly, quieter; tops risky to sell — most "double tops" become continuation in bull markets. Sell only after confirmation (break of intervening support).
- **Extended rectangle bottom** plan: long low-volatility base → buy tests of support with stop below; add on breakout pullback to old resistance; add on corrections; exit at major resistance/trend break.
- Rounded tops/bottoms (allow accumulating position); wedge tops/bottoms (volatility contraction then break).
- **Head & shoulders**: left shoulder (volume ↑), head (new high on lower volume), right shoulder (lower volume), neckline break = sell. Target = head-to-neckline distance projected down from the break.
- **Pattern failure** is itself a strong signal (trade the opposite: bull/bear traps). Change of character: when pullbacks suddenly get larger, the trend is ending.

## Episodic patterns / price shocks
Shock → bounce → sell-off → settle with declining volatility to a new level. **Backtests can "predict" shocks with hindsight → false confidence**; handle in testing (Ch 21–22).

## Price objectives (all are volatility-based)
- Horizontal range: target = resistance + (resistance − support); safer: reduce 20% or use average range.
- Channel: perpendicular width projected from breakout.
- Tops/bottoms: height of formation projected from confirmation break. Far targets are less reliable (volatility decays).
- Triangle: width of first reaction. Flag: equals move before the flag ("flagpole").
- **Rule of Seven (Sklarew)** with L = length of first leg: up targets = prior low + L·7/4, 7/3, 7/2; down = prior high − L·7/5, 7/4, 7/3 (bear moves contract).
- Practical: place target just before a resistance level; scale out at multiple targets; trend change overrides target.

## Candlesticks
- Doji (open≈close, indecision; double doji → breakout), engulfing (bullish: prev O>C, today O<prev C and C>prev O), morning/evening star, piercing line/dark cloud, hammer, hanging man, shooting star.
- Shadows: upper (white) = H−C, lower (white) = O−L; upper (black) = H−O, lower (black) = C−L; smooth each to see rising support/resistance.
- **Body momentum** = ΣB⁺ / (ΣB⁺ + |ΣB⁻|) over 14 days, B = C−O; >70% white dominates, <20% black.
- **Qstick (Chande)**: Q = SMA₈(C−O); buy when Q crosses above SMA₈(Q), sell below.
- **Floor pivots:** P = (H+L+C)/3; R1 = 2P − L; R2 = P + (H − L); S1 = 2P − H; S2 = P − (H − L). (Book misprints S1.)
- Bulkowski candles: close within lower third of bar best; bear-market candles outperform; higher volume, long wicks, tall candles outperform.

## Practical lessons
- Trends clear only in hindsight; signal always comes at point of max uncertainty. Trend systems can be right only 30–35% and still profit (big winners).
- Long-term trends (policy-driven) are most reliable; daily drift is tiny vs daily noise → weekly charts reveal it.
- Multiple independent signals converging at one price → significance.
- Practise bar-by-bar (walk-forward) without peeking.
- **Index arbitrage makes stocks move together** (S&P, GE, Exxon) → stock-level S/R often governed by the index's S/R; diversification across sectors reduced. Asian indices nearly identical after vol-adjusting.
